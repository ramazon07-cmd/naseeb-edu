In the last minute of the semi-final I tore a ligament in my knee. I heard a pop, fell on the grass, and saw my teammates run toward me while the referee blew the whistle. The ambulance took me to the hospital, and the doctor said I would not play for six months.

The first weeks were hard. I stayed at home, watched my friends practise from the window and felt sad. My coach called me every week and told me to be patient. My mother helped me with the exercises the physiotherapist gave me, and my younger brother brought me books to read. Slowly the knee became stronger and I could walk without crutches.

When I finally came back to the team in the autumn, I was slower than before, and I had to start on the bench. At every training I worked as hard as I could, and by winter I was a starter again. In our next tournament we reached the final, and although we lost, I felt proud of what the team and I had done.

This injury changed me. I learned resilience and that nothing is impossible if you never give up. Before the injury I took many things for granted, but now I understand how important it is to be grateful for what you have. I learned that setbacks are only a chance to grow, and that a strong person always stands up again. I will take this lesson with me to university and into my future career, whatever it may be, because I know that with determination I can overcome any obstacle.
