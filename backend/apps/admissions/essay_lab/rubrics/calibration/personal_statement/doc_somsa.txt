Every Sunday I am going with my mother to Chorsu bazaar to sell somsa. We wake up at four o'clock, because the tandir must be hot before the people comes. My mother make the dough the night before, and my work is to cut the onion and the meat. For fifty somsa I am cutting almost two kilos of onion, and I cry every time, my mother laugh at me.

At the bazaar we have a small table near the gate with blue tablecloth. In first months I was shy and I stood behind my mother. A old man, a taxi driver, always buyed two somsa and said me: Bolam, who does not shout, does not sell. In Uzbek we say "qo'li ochiq" about person who gives much, and my mother says it is also the seller who put the extra onion in somsa when customer is poor. So we put always more onion than the normal.

One Sunday it rains and nobody come until ten. I counted our money, it was not enough for the flour. My mother did not say nothing, she only put the tandir lid and wait. At eleven the same taxi driver comes with five friends and bought thirty somsa. We did not talk about the flour again.

We are selling somsa since I was nine. I think now that we do not sell only food. We sell a feeling that the bazaar is a home, and that a family is not a small thing, it is the people who wait with you in the rain. I want to study business to make this kind of bazaar bigger, but never colder.
