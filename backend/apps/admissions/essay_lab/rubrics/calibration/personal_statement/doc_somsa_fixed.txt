Every Sunday I go with my mother to Chorsu bazaar to sell somsa. We wake up at four o'clock, because the tandir must be hot before the people come. My mother makes the dough the night before, and my job is to cut the onion and the meat. For fifty somsa I cut almost two kilos of onion, and I cry every time, and my mother laughs at me.

At the bazaar we have a small table near the gate with a blue tablecloth. In the first months I was shy and I stood behind my mother. An old man, a taxi driver, always bought two somsa and told me: "Bolam, who does not shout, does not sell." In Uzbek we say "qo'li ochiq" about a person who gives a lot, and my mother says it also means the seller who puts an extra onion in the somsa when a customer is poor. So we always put in more onion than normal.

One Sunday it rained and nobody came until ten. I counted our money, and it was not enough for the flour. My mother did not say anything; she only put the lid on the tandir and waited. At eleven the same taxi driver came with five friends and bought thirty somsa. We did not talk about the flour again.

We have been selling somsa since I was nine. I think now that we do not sell only food. We sell a feeling that the bazaar is a home, and that a family is not a small thing, it is the people who wait with you in the rain. I want to study business to make this kind of bazaar bigger, but never colder.
