My father fixes tires in a shed behind our house in Samarkand, and since I was twelve I have held the flashlight. I know the sound of a slow leak better than I know the sound of my own alarm. Last summer a customer paid him with a bag of apricots because the machine had broken again and he could not charge the usual price. I wrote the date in the notebook where he tracks debts, 140 entries, half of them in pencil. I told him he should print a price list. He laughed and said people here pay with trust, and I did not know whether to be angry at him or at the notebook. I still do not know. I am studying economics because I want to understand why his way works for forty families and loses money every month. Maybe it should not be fixed. I want to be able to say why.
