Last winter our school heating broke for eleven days, and the physics room was the coldest room in the building. Our teacher, Dilfuza opa, refused to cancel lessons. Instead she made us measure the temperature every hour with a thermometer taped to the window frame and plot it on the board.

By the fourth day I noticed that the room lost heat faster on the days when the corridor door stayed open, not when it was windiest outside. I asked if we could test it. We kept the door shut for one day and open for the next, and the difference was almost two degrees by noon.

It was a small experiment, and the heating came back before we could repeat it. I still think about it when people say physics is only in textbooks. Nobody told us the answer first.
