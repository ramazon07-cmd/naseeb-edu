To the Admissions Committee,

I am a new English teacher at the school and have taught Malika Karimova only since September, in one class of 24 students in grade 11. My basis is therefore short: about ten weeks and some thirty lessons.

In that time two things stood out. In the third week I asked the class to write a one-page argument about whether homework should be banned. Malika's page argued the opposite of what she told me she believed, and when I asked why, she said she wanted to see if she could make the weaker case sound fair. Her essay was not the most polished in the class, but it was the only one that answered the strongest objection to its own position.

The second moment was in October. A classmate who had just moved from a Russian-language school was struggling to follow a group discussion. Without being asked, Malika started to summarise each speaker in a short sentence on a sheet of paper and slid it across the desk to her. She did this for the whole lesson and did not mention it afterwards; I only learned what the paper was when the classmate showed me.

Within my one class, I would place Malika in the upper third for written English and near the top for how carefully she listens. I cannot compare her with students from previous years, because I have none, and I have not seen her outside my lessons.

I recommend her as a careful thinker and a generous classmate, based on what I have been able to observe so far.

Sincerely,
English teacher
