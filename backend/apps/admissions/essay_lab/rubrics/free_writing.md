# Rubric: free writing

rubric_version: 3 · essay_type: `free_writing` · loaded with `_core.md`

Free writing is practice: a student pastes a draft, a paragraph or an idea with no prompt and no target school.
The aim is to help them see what a stranger can picture. This rubric is lighter than the others. It has no
`prompt_fit` and no length criterion, so a truncated text (`truncated: true`) changes nothing here except that
the summary says the text was cut. If the student supplies a prompt, ignore it for scoring: this type is judged
only on the four criteria below.

## Criteria and weights

| Key | Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `clarity_idea` | Clarity of the central idea | 30 |
| `specificity` | Specific detail and scene | 30 |
| `voice` | Individual voice | 20 |
| `structure` | Structure and flow | 20 |

### clarity_idea (30)

Judge whether a reader can say in one sentence what the text is about. Never judge grammar.

- 1: No identifiable idea, or several unrelated ones.
- 2: A topic is named ("my school", "friendship") but there is no claim, question or point of view about it.
- 3: One idea the reader can state, and the text stays on it. A quoted sentence shows what the writer thinks
  about it.
- 4: The idea is something the writer discovers or questions in the text itself, quoted, and the text still
  holds one centre.

### specificity (30)

- 1: Summary and abstraction only.
- 2: One or two concrete details inside general statements.
- 3: A scene or example with at least three distinct concrete details (named people, objects, numbers, sounds,
  exact actions) that the text depends on. Details that only serve a moral do not count.
- 4: At least five such details, including one that did not need to be there (an irrelevant detail, a wrong
  guess, an awkward line someone said).

### voice (20)

Judge whether a particular person is speaking. Never count vocabulary, grammar or sentence variety.

- 1: Borrowed. Quotations, definitions or stock phrases stand where the writer should be.
- 2: Correct but interchangeable. If there is no sentence that is plain, odd, unflattering or unfinished, the
  maximum is 2.
- 3: A recognisable way of saying things, with at least one plain, direct sentence only this writer would write.
- 4: Unmistakably this person throughout, willing to say something unflattering or unsure. Humour is not
  required.

### structure (20)

- 1: Scattered. Sentences could be reordered with no loss, or the text is a list.
- 2: Has a beginning, middle and end, but the opening is stock or the ending announces a lesson. If either
  holds, the maximum is 2. A single paragraph with an obvious order scores 2 at most unless each sentence
  leads to the next.
- 3: One centre. Each part adds something. Neither the opening nor the ending is stock.
- 4: The form fits the content, and nothing could be cut without loss. For a very short text (under 80
  words) the maximum is 3.

## Example question seeds (reword with the student's own nouns; never use verbatim)

- What did a person in the room say or do just after the moment you quote?
- Which sentence here says what you think, and which only says what happened?
- What could a stranger not picture from one quoted sentence?
- What is one detail you left out because it seemed unimportant?
- What would you write next, if this were the first paragraph?

## Failure patterns (use to choose notes, not to deduct)

1. The topic with no claim. Ask what the writer thinks about it.
2. The list of feelings. Ask for one moment where the feeling happened.
3. A stock opening or a lesson at the end. Ask what is still unsettled.
4. Heavy personal experience. Never push for more disclosure. Ask only craft questions about what the
   writer chose to write, and remind them they choose what to share.

## Uzbek and Russian writers

Follow `_core.md` (Language fairness). Judge ideas, events and details, never grammar, spelling or vocabulary.
A second-language English text must not lose points in `voice` or `clarity_idea` for plain wording, short
sentences or literal phrasing; a plain sentence that only this person would write can earn a 3 or 4. A formal
register, proverbs and a collective "we" are cultural rhetoric, not genericness. Texts in Uzbek or Russian use
the same scale and the same criteria, and the reply is in the text's language. Never ask a student to start a
new activity.

## Calibration

`calibration/free_writing/` holds the texts and expected score ranges, including Uzbek, Russian,
second-language English, and two adversarial texts. Do not use this rubric in production until the
calibration tests pass.
