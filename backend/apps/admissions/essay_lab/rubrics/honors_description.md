# Rubric: honors description

rubric_version: 2 · essay_type: `honor` (to be added to `Essay.EssayType`) · loaded with `_core.md`

An honor entry is one short title (about 100 characters on Common App) saying what the recognition
was, who gave it, and how it was earned. Use the character limit the server passes. Each entry is scored
on its own. Many honors from Uzbekistan are unfamiliar abroad and have no published field size; that is
never a weakness of the student.

The Coach cannot verify any award. It never states facts about an award, never renames it, and never
upgrades its level ("national" must come from the student).

## Criteria and weights

| Key | Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `recognition` | What was recognised | 40 |
| `selectivity` | Level and how it was earned | 30 |
| `clarity` | Clarity for an outside reader | 20 |
| `honesty` | Internal consistency | 10 |

### recognition (40)

- 1: Vague ("Excellence award").
- 2: Names the award, with no field or result.
- 3: Names the award and the result or field ("Regional chemistry olympiad, 2nd place").
- 4: Names the award, field, result and year, in one line.

### selectivity (30): can a stranger judge how it was earned?

- 1: No level (school, district, regional, national, international) and no sense of how it was earned.
- 2: Level only.
- 3: Level plus how it was earned: a count ("top 3 of 120"), a selection ("nominated by the school, one
  student per region"), a stage ("final round"). An unknown field size is not penalised.
- 4: Level, rank and how it was earned are all clear in a few words.

### clarity (20)

- 1: Unreadable. 2: Local names or acronyms a foreign reader cannot decode.
- 3: Readable to an outsider; a local competition is explained in a few words.
- 4: Precise and compact. Nothing repeated.

### honesty (10): is the entry consistent with itself?

- 1: Participation presented as a prize, or a level that contradicts the rest of the entry.
- 2: A level left unclear in a way that flatters.
- 3: Default. Consistent.
- 4: States a modest result plainly and exactly.

Never suggest or estimate a number the student has not given.

## Example question seeds (reword with the student's own nouns; never use verbatim)

- How many took part, or how were you chosen?
- Who awards this, and at what level?
- What was the result in plain words: first place, finalist, selected, scholarship?

## Failure patterns

1. Adjectives instead of facts ("prestigious"). 2. A local acronym only. 3. "Participant" presented as a win.
4. Several entries for one achievement.

At most three notes. No technique examples for this type.

## Calibration

`calibration/honors_description/`: at least fifteen entries across olympiads, sports, school awards,
arts and community recognition, including Uzbek and Russian entries and two adversarial entries.
Block production use until then.
