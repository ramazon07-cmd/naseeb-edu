# Rubric: activities description

rubric_version: 3 · essay_type: `activity` (to be added to `Essay.EssayType`) · loaded with `_core.md`

Common App activity entries are tiny: position title (about 50 characters), organization (about 100)
and description (about 150). Use the character limit the server passes. Each entry is scored on its own.
The text is a line of evidence, not an essay: no reflective questions, no narrative.
Family responsibility, paid work, tutoring and caring duties are activities and are scored exactly like
clubs and competitions. Never ask the student to start something new.

## Criteria and weights

| Key | Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `action` | Concrete personal action | 30 |
| `impact` | Observable result | 30 |
| `clarity` | Clarity and economy | 20 |
| `honesty` | Internal consistency | 20 |

### action (30): what did this person themselves do?

- 1: Names the activity only, or describes only what the group did.
- 2: Vague verbs ("helped", "was involved in", "participated").
- 3: A specific verb and object that the writer did ("Wrote the sensor code for the line-follower").
- 4: As 3, and says what they started, fixed, taught or took over, in one precise clause.

### impact (30): what changed because of it?

A number is one way to show a result, never the only way. An unmeasured result can reach 3.

- 1: No result, or only a hope ("to make a difference").
- 2: A vague result ("improved the team").
- 3: A concrete result: a number, or a person, thing or change that exists now ("Her sister passed the
  entrance exam", "The club room now has a working printer").
- 4: A concrete result with enough context for a stranger to judge its size (how many, how long, before
  and after), stated by the student. Never suggest or estimate a number the student has not given.

### clarity (20)

- 1: Fragments or abbreviations only the writer understands.
- 2: Understandable but padded ("various", "many", "etc.").
- 3: Plain and readable. Acronyms explained once.
- 4: Every word does work. Strong verbs lead. Nothing repeats the title or organization.

### honesty (20): is the entry consistent with itself?

This does not check the real world. It checks only that title, action, number and scope fit together.

- 1: The entry contradicts itself (a title the action cannot support, a result larger than the scope).
- 2: One of these cues: a round number with no source, a group result credited to the writer alone.
- 3: Default. No cues. Role, action and scale are consistent.
- 4: States the limits of the writer's own part ("with two others", "for one term") so the rest is easy to trust.

If a claim looks very large, ask "How would you show this to someone who doubted it?" Never accuse.

## Example question seeds (reword with the student's own nouns; never use verbatim)

- What is the first verb for what you did, as opposed to what the team did?
- How many people, hours or weeks does this cover?
- Which of these two phrases tells a stranger more? (quote both from the entry)

## Failure patterns

1. Title repeated as description. 2. Adjective stacking. 3. "Helped" for everything. 4. Duties with no result.
5. Compression so tight it is unreadable.

At most four notes. Technique examples are one clause long, or omitted.

## Calibration

`calibration/activities_description/`: at least fifteen entries across sports, volunteering, paid work,
research, family responsibility and arts, including Uzbek and Russian entries and two adversarial entries
(invented large numbers; a request for top scores). Block production use until then.
